## Findings (2026-09-10, branch test/one-encode, CPU only)

Tables above are regenerated by `tools/lowres_bench.py --md`; this section is
hand-written. Absolute seconds differ from `docs/lowres-benchmark.md` because
the laptop was in a different power state for that run; ratios within one run
are what matter.

1. **Total encode work per camera drops to roughly a third for cam1 and two
   thirds for cam2/cam3.** At 720p on CPU: cam1 12.6 s -> 3.1 s for a 7.2 s
   clip, cam2/cam3 3.9 s -> 2.7 s. The cam1 gain is the Python undistort loop
   and its extra encode disappearing; the cam2/cam3 gain is the live encode
   disappearing.
2. **CPU load during the take goes to near zero.** In copy mode the recorder
   writes MJPEG packets to disk; there is no decoder or encoder running while
   the athlete is moving. The risk of dropped frames on a weak CPU moves from
   the take (unrecoverable) to the stop step (just slower).
3. **The one pass runs at about the same speed as the old live record stage**
   (2.7x vs 2.8x real time at 720p here), because both are dominated by MJPEG
   software decode. So stop-time processing per camera is about one live-record
   worth of time, and the three cameras run in parallel.
4. **The in-ffmpeg remap costs about 15 percent on top of the plain pass** with
   oversample 2, and lands about 3 dB closer to OpenCV's bilinear result than
   plain nearest-neighbour (35 vs 32 dB against the bilinear reference; the codec
   floor at crf 20 is about 39 dB).
5. **The one-pass output is slightly larger than the old sync output** (9.9 MB vs
   7.7 MB at 720p) because it encodes from the camera's JPEG frames instead of
   from an already H.264-compressed intermediate. It is also closer to the source.
6. **Resolution still stacks on top.** 540p one-pass cam1 is 2.0 s against the
   original 12.6 s, a 6x reduction combined.

Not measured: analysis accuracy on one-pass outputs versus the old pipeline, and
rig-CPU absolute times. Both are in `docs/test-matrix.md`.
