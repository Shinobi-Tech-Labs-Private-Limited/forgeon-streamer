## Findings (2026-09-10, branch test/low-res)

The tables above are regenerated by `tools/lowres_bench.py --md`; this section is
hand-written and should be updated when the tables change.

1. **The live take is bottlenecked by MJPEG decode, not by H.264 encode.**
   At 1280x720 the record stage runs at the same speed with NVENC as with
   libx264 (1.1x real time either way), so the encoder is not the limit; the
   software MJPEG decode is. That is the cost the camera's output format
   imposes, and it scales with pixel count: 640x360 decodes about 3.6x faster.
   The rig's `mjpeg_cuvid` decode path (needs a working CUDA driver) attacks
   the same bottleneck without dropping resolution.
2. **Post-stop time scales roughly with pixel count.** Sync re-encode and the
   cam1 undistort loop both drop to about half at 854x480 and to about a
   quarter at 640x360. The undistort loop is slower than real time at 720p
   even on a 16-thread laptop (0.5x), so on the rig a one-minute take spends
   more than two minutes in that loop alone.
3. **Upload size scales a little faster than pixel count** because the encoder
   spends fewer bits per pixel on a downscaled frame: 54% at 540p, 37% at 480p,
   14% at 360p (libx264 rows).
4. **NVENC at the rig's settings (`-cq 19/20`) writes files 2.5x to 3x larger
   than libx264 at `-crf 18/20`.** The quality is higher too (PSNR 43.7 vs
   39.3 dB), but if upload size is the concern, the NVENC `-cq` value needs
   retuning; the two encoders' quality scales are not equivalent.
5. **Quality cost of downscaling.** Against the 720p source, 540p loses about
   3.6 dB PSNR and 0.025 SSIM, 480p about 5.2 dB and 0.042 SSIM, 360p about
   9 dB and 0.13 SSIM. 360p is visibly soft; 540p and 480p are the candidates
   worth putting through the analysis pipeline.

What this does not answer: whether the pose/ball analysis still hits its
accuracy target on the 540p and 480p outputs. The bench writes
`sync_<res>_<enc>.mp4` for exactly that test.

## How to try it on the rig

1. On each camera Pi, confirm the sensor advertises the MJPEG mode:
   `v4l2-ctl -d /dev/video0 --list-formats-ext | grep -A3 MJPG` and check that
   the target size is listed at 90 fps.
2. Launch the app with `RIG_CAPTURE_RES=960x540` (or `854x480`) in the
   environment. The camera bootstrap passes it to every `v4l2rtspserver`.
3. Existing 1280x720 calibrations are rescaled automatically for the cam1
   undistortion (same aspect ratio only). Recalibrating at the new size is
   still the cleaner option for a production rollout.
4. Record a take and compare `sync_manifest.json` timings and the uploaded
   file sizes against a 1280x720 take on the same rig.
